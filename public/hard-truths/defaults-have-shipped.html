The Defaults Have Shipped

Vlad Cealicu // 25 November 2026

They are coming after you, and they are doing it for free or cheap.

> EPISODE 25 // OFFLINE-READY NOTEBOOKLM AUDIO
00:00 / --:-- DOWNLOAD

On Tuesday 24 November 2026 David Singleton, Meta's VP of engineering for AI products and a former CTO of Stripe, posted that Muse is in the UK [1]. Muse is Meta's personal AI agent, and it sends your emails, books your travel and pays at the checkout, you can talk to it in WhatsApp, and it's free for most of what people need [2]. I read his post that night, and it's happening, what I predicted in the manifesto and the first few Hard Truths posts, and it's not even been a year.

> What I said

Why We Build is dated 18 December 2025 and said we were done with promises, because "Promises are broken in board meetings", and that a design is hostile if the vendor can brick you. Inflection followed on 31 December 2025 and said that Apple, Google and Meta were watching the same trend lines and were "not going to cede this territory", that their answer would be "local" AI that phones home, with privacy marketing on top of telemetry, that personal AI assistants were the last layer left to capture (I called it the cognitive self, the "extraction layer that captures reasoning itself"), and that the cost of free services is your agency and not only your data. The threat I used as the example was Apple shipping a "$299 home AI device", and the post ended with "The defaults haven't shipped yet."

> What shipped

Meta launched Muse in the US on 8 September 2026, under nine months after the manifesto, and it has been downloaded more than 10 million times there [3]. Apple announced HomeView on 13 October 2026, a tablet for the home that recognises who in the household is using it, at $299 and shipping from 13 November [4]. Google made its own agent, Tasks in Gemini, free in six countries on 17 November [5], and a week after that Muse arrived here.

They are coming after you, and they are doing it for free or cheap. They want how you think, what you buy and every login you have, and Meta was the first of them to give an agent away, but they will all do it.

> Verify, don't trust

You should not listen to their marketing, you should look at what the terms and conditions say and at their track record, and the same goes for what I wrote. Both of my pages have been edited for wording since December, and the versions from then are in the site's git history [6]. I had Apple's price right and the timing wrong, because Inflection had the default being set in the first half of 2026 and HomeView shipped in November. I also had Apple going first, and by the time its box was in the shops Meta had been giving an agent away for two months with no box at all, because, in David Singleton's words, "Every Muse runs on its own computer in the cloud" [1].

Meta's marketing says that each Muse gets its own virtual machine with a second agent called Sentinel approving anything that leaves it [7], that none of your conversations go to the advertising systems [2], and that the money will come from a small fee on transactions, which is what Mark Zuckerberg said at Connect on 23 September 2026 [8]. I don't believe the money part, and I think it will come from ads, let's not kid ourselves, we're smarter than that. Advertising was $63.06 billion of Meta's $64.60 billion of revenue in the third quarter of 2026 [9], and the ads business will keep doing what it does, because it's free money for them.

The terms promise less than the marketing does, because the Muse privacy policy keeps only two things out of the ad systems, your conversations and the data on your virtual machine, while the same policy says Meta collects how you use Muse (the features you use, the websites it visits, the actions it takes and the transactions it makes) and points to the main Meta privacy policy for what that is used for [10]. Meta's own engineering post says that when Muse browses it appears as your activity, so a shop it visits for you can use that visit to show you an ad on Instagram, and that limited data leaves the machine for inference and telemetry [7], which is what Inflection calls privacy marketing sitting on top of telemetry requirements. Your conversations train Meta's models unless you switch that off [11]. The fee is to be paid by the merchant, not by you [8], and I think a shop paying Meta when Meta's agent spends your money there is advertising.

Their track record is a history of changing the terms and conditions, or ignoring them, to make more money. In December 2012, the year it bought Instagram, new Instagram terms let a business pay to show your name and your photos in sponsored content without paying you, and they were taken back three days later after the uproar [12]. When it bought WhatsApp in 2014 it told the European Commission it couldn't reliably match WhatsApp accounts to Facebook ones, in August 2016 WhatsApp's new terms linked phone numbers to Facebook identities, and in May 2017 the Commission fined Facebook €110 million for the misleading answer [13]. The phone numbers people gave Facebook for two-factor security were used for advertising, which was one of the charges settled by the $5 billion penalty the FTC imposed in July 2019 [14]. WhatsApp's founders vowed to keep it free of ads, and the ads arrived in June 2025 [15]. And on 16 December 2025, two days before the date on the manifesto, Meta started using what people say to Meta AI to target ads, with no way to opt out (the UK, the EU and South Korea were left out) [16].

The Muse privacy policy says nothing about how it can be changed [10], and the manifesto has the line for that, "Promises get broken in board meetings, so I'd rather build systems where breaking one changes nothing."

> The three tests

The manifesto has three tests, and today Muse fails all three. Local-first says that if it can't run without their servers, you're a tenant, and Muse is a computer in Meta's cloud. True ownership says that if you don't hold the keys, you don't own the data, and Meta's own security post says its policies restrict staff access but do not prevent Meta from accessing the data when it needs to support, secure or operate the service [7]. A version encrypted with a key only you hold was promised for later in 2026 and hasn't shipped [11]. No kill switch says a design is hostile if a vendor can brick your device, and Meta can switch Muse off, or change what the free tier buys, without asking you.

In The Expensive Part Was Never the AI, in June 2026, I wrote that "AI only looked cheap because it was subsidised, and you were the land being taken." I think Muse's free tier is the same subsidy, and that it lasts until the habit is formed.

If Meta ships the key that only you hold and makes it the default, Muse passes the second of those three tests, and it would still be their computer.

> What I'm doing about it

LocalGhost is the alternative I'm building, a personal AI that runs on a computer under your desk, where you hold the keys and no company can switch it off, and What the Box Does Now has what's built and what each part costs. It isn't free, it isn't in WhatsApp, and it's still a lot more work to set up than opening an app. Inflection says that privacy alone has never won a consumer market, and that the job is to exist as a credible alternative, because even if most people never switch, a real option limits the worst behaviour, and that's still the job.

If you install Muse, read the terms, switch the training off and look at what it remembers about you. And if you work on Muse and think I've got any of this wrong, or you're building the alternative, reach out at info@localghost.ai. [ localghost.ai // hard-truths ]
