> SECTION_01

Core Identity

The shelter terminal. A green phosphor CRT out of a 1950s idea of the future, running a cypherpunk OS. The screen is the hacker terminal of the 1990s, the furniture around it is retro-futurist civil defence, the bulletins pinned beside it are dry, and the bunker is a box under your desk with your own life in it.

The retro look goes back to when a computer was a tool you owned and could open, and it's built with modern restraint, so the text stays readable and the effects stay out of the way.

No borrowed mascots, logos or names from any game or film, however close the mood.

The Positioning

LocalGhost moves the AI, the data and the memory built from it off someone else's servers and onto a box you own, which answers your phone and gives everyone else the same 503 a dead server would.

> THE CORE PROMISE

[1]
Privacy through architecture, not promises. We can't see your data because the system prevents it.
[2]
Ownership through atoms, not accounts. You own hardware. Hardware doesn't have terms of service.
[3]
Sovereignty through code, not trust. Verify, don't believe.

Usage Rules

Each colour is a status. Terminal Green is what runs and what we stand for, Amber is what's planned and not built yet, and Warning Red is what we reject (surveillance, rent-seeking, enshittification). A reader should be able to tell built from planned by colour alone, so don't use any of the three for decoration.

Visual Effects

Texture that makes the page read as a lit tube without getting in the way of reading it. The constant effects are faint, and the loud ones are easter eggs that show up rarely, for a moment, and never for anyone whose system asks for reduced motion.

SCANLINES
> GLOW

CRT FLICKER
1px solid
SHARP BORDERS

Effect Specifications

Scanlines. Repeating linear gradient, 2px transparent / 2px rgba(0,0,0,0.1). Applied as fixed overlay across entire viewport.

Terminal Glow. Box-shadow and text-shadow on green elements. text-shadow: 0 0 5px var(--terminal-dim)

Phosphor wash and vignette. A faint green radial wash behind the content, brightest a third of the way down, and a dark vignette at the edges of the viewport, both fixed and pointer-transparent. They're in base.css, so every page gets them.

Flicker. Randomised, slow animation (6s+ duration) inside the terminal only. Subtle opacity changes, enough to feel alive.

Easter eggs. /js/phosphor.js lets the tube misbehave now and then, an interference band rolling down, a flash, the vertical hold slipping, or a line burning in faintly. The first comes 30 to 90 seconds after the page opens, then one every one to three minutes, never while the tab is hidden. Typing ghost outside a text field puts up PLEASE STAND BY.

Decoration stays out of the text. Flavour lines like the footer's END OF TRANSMISSION live in CSS, so screen readers, crawlers and the markdown twins of each page get the content and none of the set dressing.

Borders. Always 1px. Sharp corners (0 or 4px max border-radius for code blocks). No drop shadows. No blur effects on cards.

Component Rules

Buttons. Square brackets in label text. 1px border. No background until hover. Ghost variant uses terminal green.

Cards. 1px border, no border-radius. Cards are cells, they feel like terminal panes, not material design cards.

Terminal chrome. The dot-buttons (red/yellow/green) are interactive easter eggs, not decoration. Close triggers Matrix rain. Maximise expands the terminal.

> SECTION_08

Voice & Tone

The writing guidelines are the rules for every word on the site, and this section is how they meet the look. The short version is someone who built the thing walking a sceptical peer through it, plainly, with the numbers, saying what isn't done yet, and with a dry joke where one lands.

How the voice meets the look

Plain prose, loud furniture. The terminal chrome, the placards and the boot screen carry the retro-futurist mood, so the sentences don't have to. Body copy stays first person and specific.

Built in green, planned in amber. Anything not running today gets said in the copy and marked in amber. The look should never make a plan read like a feature.

Code as proof. A claim about privacy names the mechanism (the 503, the certificate, the TPM seal) or links the code, rather than asking for trust.

Dark humour. "At least this 404 doesn't phone home." Civil-defence deadpan about the absurd parts of the status quo, and about ourselves, never at the reader.

> SECTION_09

Design Principles

> INTERACTION PHILOSOPHY

[1]
Easter eggs over empty polish. Curiosity gets rewarded. The home terminal takes commands, the snake game is about what a model eats, and the tube misbehaves now and then if you stay long enough.
[2]
No dark patterns. The waitlist modal doesn't collect email and the donate modal shows a raw ETH address, with nothing in between to nudge you.
[3]
Nothing interrupts. No auto-play and no pop-ups. The easter eggs never take focus, and the one that covers the screen comes only when you type for it.
[4]
Respect for the reader. No hand-holding. No FAQs that start with "What is a computer?" You're technical, you're skeptical, and you've been burned before.

Emotional Register

Controlled anger. The copy is political but not preachy. Angry at systems, not people. Channels frustration into action.

Quiet confidence. We don't beg. We don't compare ourselves to competitors. We state what we've built and let the architecture speak.

Dark humour. The gallows wit of people who've seen the machine and chosen to build the exit. Never cruel, always knowing.
